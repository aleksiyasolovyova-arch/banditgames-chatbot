# Connect Four — Rules and Strategy Guide

## 1. Overview
Connect Four is a two-player abstract strategy game in which players take turns dropping colored discs into a vertical grid. The objective is to be the first player to form a straight line of four of their own discs.

The game is deterministic, turn-based, and features perfect information, making it suitable for strategic planning and tactical foresight.

---

## 2. Equipment and Setup
- A vertical grid with **7 columns and 6 rows**
- **42 discs** in total, split evenly between two players
- Each player chooses a color (commonly red and yellow)

### Initial Setup
1. The grid is placed upright and empty.
2. Players decide who goes first (often randomly).
3. Players alternate turns throughout the game.

---

## 3. Basic Rules of Play

### Turn Structure
1. On your turn, select one of the seven columns.
2. Drop one disc into the chosen column.
3. The disc falls to the **lowest available space** in that column.
4. If a column is completely full, it cannot be selected.

### Winning Condition
A player wins immediately when they form **four of their discs in a straight line**:
- Horizontally
- Vertically
- Diagonally (both directions)

### Draw Condition
If all 42 spaces are filled and no player has formed four in a row, the game ends in a draw.

---

## 4. Strategic Concepts

### 4.1 Center Control
The center column (column 4) is the most powerful position:
- It allows connections in **more directions** than edge columns.
- Strong opening strategy often involves playing in the center early.

### 4.2 Threat Creation
A *threat* is a position where you will win on your next turn unless your opponent blocks.

Advanced players aim to:
- Create **multiple threats simultaneously**
- Force the opponent into defensive play

### 4.3 Vertical Strategy
Vertical connections are:
- Easier to block
- Often predictable

They are useful early, but rarely decisive at high levels unless combined with diagonal or horizontal threats.

### 4.4 Horizontal Strategy
Horizontal lines require:
- Careful planning
- Disc support underneath

They are stronger in the **middle rows** where flexibility is higher.

### 4.5 Diagonal Strategy
Diagonal wins are:
- The hardest to see
- The hardest to defend against

Mastering diagonals is essential for advanced play.

---

## 5. Forcing Moves and Traps

### 5.1 Double Threats
A double threat occurs when a player creates **two winning moves at once**.
The opponent can only block one, guaranteeing victory.

### 5.2 Zugzwang Positions
Certain positions force the opponent to play a move that gives you a winning opportunity, even if they play optimally.

---

## 6. First-Move Advantage
Connect Four is a **solved game**:
- With perfect play, the **first player can always force a win**
- This is achieved through optimal center control and threat sequencing

---

## 7. Summary
- Simple rules, deep strategy
- Emphasis on foresight and spatial reasoning
- Ideal for both casual and competitive play
