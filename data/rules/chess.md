# Chess — Rules and Strategic Foundations

## 1. Overview
Chess is a two-player strategic board game played on an 8×8 grid. It is a game of perfect information with no randomness, emphasizing planning, calculation, and positional understanding.

The objective is to **checkmate** the opponent’s king.

---

## 2. Equipment and Setup

### The Chessboard
- 8 ranks (rows) and 8 files (columns)
- Alternating light and dark squares
- Each player starts with a light square in the bottom-right corner

### Pieces per Player
- 1 King
- 1 Queen
- 2 Rooks
- 2 Bishops
- 2 Knights
- 8 Pawns

---

## 3. Basic Rules of Play

### Turn Order
- White moves first
- Players alternate turns
- On each turn, a player moves exactly one piece

---

## 4. Piece Movement Rules

### King
- Moves one square in any direction
- Cannot move into check

### Queen
- Moves any number of squares horizontally, vertically, or diagonally

### Rook
- Moves any number of squares horizontally or vertically

### Bishop
- Moves any number of squares diagonally
- Always stays on the same color square

### Knight
- Moves in an L-shape (two squares in one direction, then one perpendicular)
- Can jump over other pieces

### Pawn
- Moves forward one square
- Can move two squares on its first move
- Captures diagonally
- Promotes upon reaching the opposite end of the board

---

## 5. Special Rules

### Castling
A simultaneous move involving the king and rook:
- King moves two squares toward a rook
- Rook moves to the square the king crossed
Conditions:
- Neither piece has moved before
- No pieces between them
- King does not pass through check

### En Passant
A special pawn capture possible immediately after a pawn moves two squares forward and passes an enemy pawn.

### Promotion
When a pawn reaches the final rank, it must be promoted to:
- Queen, Rook, Bishop, or Knight

---

## 6. Check, Checkmate, and Draws

### Check
- The king is under immediate threat
- Must be resolved immediately

### Checkmate
- The king is in check and cannot escape
- The game ends immediately

### Draw Conditions
- Stalemate
- Threefold repetition
- Fifty-move rule
- Insufficient material
- Mutual agreement

---

## 7. Strategic Phases of the Game

### 7.1 Opening
Goals:
- Control the center
- Develop minor pieces
- Castle early
- Avoid unnecessary pawn moves

### 7.2 Middlegame
Focus:
- Tactical combinations
- King safety
- Piece coordination
- Attacking and defending weaknesses

### 7.3 Endgame
Characteristics:
- Fewer pieces
- King becomes an active piece
- Precise calculation is critical

---

## 8. Fundamental Strategic Concepts

### 8.1 Piece Value
Approximate values:
- Pawn = 1
- Knight/Bishop = 3
- Rook = 5
- Queen = 9

These values guide exchanges but are context-dependent.

### 8.2 King Safety
A safe king is essential:
- Castle early
- Avoid exposing the king to open files

### 8.3 Pawn Structure
Pawn formations determine:
- Open and closed positions
- Weak squares
- Long-term strategy

---

## 9. Tactical Motifs

Common tactics include:
- Forks
- Pins
- Skewers
- Discovered attacks
- Back-rank mates

Tactical awareness often decides games.

---

## 10. Strategic Planning
Strong players:
- Identify long-term plans
- Improve piece activity
- Restrict opponent options
- Convert advantages gradually

---

## 11. Summary
Chess combines deep strategy with tactical precision.
Mastery requires:
- Pattern recognition
- Calculation
- Long-term planning
- Psychological resilience
